A Biological Heritage at Risk

Unlike African elephants where both males and females carry ivory, only a small fraction of Asian elephant bulls in Sri Lanka possess tusks. Centuries of colonial trophy hunting eliminated a vast portion of tusk-bearing genes. Today, every surviving wild tusker like Thirappane Agbo and Podi Dala Poottuwa carries irreplaceable evolutionary heritage.

Human-elephant conflict, illegal high-voltage farm wires, and deadly jaw-bombs (Hakka Patas) pose an existential threat. Wanali serves as both a historical registry and a call for proactive wildlife corridors and peaceful coexistence.
