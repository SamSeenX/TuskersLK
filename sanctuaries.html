Living Corridors: Elephant Sanctuaries of Sri Lanka

ශ්‍රී ලංකාවේ අලි අභයභූමි සහ සංක්‍රමණික තීර

Sri Lanka contains one of the densest wild Asian elephant populations on the globe. Across its dry-zone lowlands and ancient river basins, a network of protected parks and connecting scrublands sustains the great herds.

Six Vital Elephant Basins

Kala Wewa Basin

North-central Sri Lanka premier wild tusker haven. Renowned for freshwater grasslands that sustained legendary patriarchs like Revatha, Barana, and Walagamba.

Udawalawe National Park

An expansive grassland basin surrounding the Walawe reservoir, sustaining hundreds of year-round resident elephants and the Elephant Transit Home.

Minneriya & Kaudulla

Site of the world-famous seasonal Gathering, where up to four hundred wild elephants convene annually on the exposed ancient reservoir bed.

Wasgamuwa & Riverine Woods

A rugged wilderness bordered by the Mahaweli River, famous for dense riverine forests and massive wild patriarchs like Sumedha.

Yala & Ruhuna Coastline

Coastal scrublands and lagoons in southeastern Sri Lanka, home to renowned iconic coastal tuskers like Gemunu and Nandamitra.

Somawathiya Floodplains

Vast marshlands and villus along the lower Mahaweli delta, sheltering solitary giants including Sando and Mahasen.

Why Corridors Matter More than Static Parks

Elephants are a wide-ranging, migratory species. Static national park borders drawn on a map cannot contain a herd's seasonal search for water and food.

Securing recognized jungle corridors between national parks enables healthy genetic exchange and allows herds to move naturally without crossing into cultivated village plots.
