Deceased Wild Tusker c. 1970 – 2021 (Aged approx. 50)

Kala Wewa Revatha

කලාවැවේ රේවත

Archival Field Dossier

Roaming Territory Kala Wewa and Balalu Wewa Sanctuary
Habitat Sanctuary Kala Wewa National Park and Balalu Wewa wetland reserve
Geographic Coordinates 8.0167 N, 80.5333 E
Physical Anatomy & Tusks Dark black pigmentation, thick downward-sloping symmetrical tusks, uniquely calm disposition around humans
Fate and Cause of Death Electrocuted in March 2021 by an illegal electric fence erected around private farmland bordering the sanctuary.

Significance and Cultural Legacy

The beloved gentle giant of the Kala Wewa tank ecosystem. Thousands of nature photographers and local families visited Kala Wewa each dry season specifically to view Revatha feeding peacefully among lotus beds.

"Sri Lankan tuskers represent an ancient biological and cultural heritage. Only a rare percentage of Sri Lankan Asian elephant bulls develop majestic ivory tusks, making each individual a living national treasure."
